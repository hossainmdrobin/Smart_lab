Website Development Brief
Laboratory of Algorithmic Life Sciences
Dhaka, Bangladesh
Developer-ready requirements for the design, development, launch, and handover of the laboratory website.
1. Project overview
Develop a professional, modern, and easy-to-maintain academic website for the Laboratory of Algorithmic Life Sciences, based in Dhaka, Bangladesh.
Computational drug design.
Density functional theory (DFT).
Molecular docking.
Molecular dynamics (MD) simulation.
Network pharmacology.
Machine learning.
Artificial intelligence in drug discovery.
The website should communicate our research identity, introduce our team, showcase publications and projects, and help prospective collaborators and researchers contact us. It should feel like a credible scientific laboratory website—not a pharmaceutical sales website, hospital website, or generic software company website.
Any information not supplied by the laboratory must remain a clearly identified placeholder during development and must be removed or approved before publication.
2. Main objectives and audiences
Objectives
Establish a clear online identity for the laboratory.
Present research areas in an understandable and scientifically appropriate way.
Showcase verified publications, ongoing projects, and research outputs.
Introduce laboratory members and their expertise.

Allow designated laboratory members to update content without coding.
Intended audiences
Academic researchers and potential collaborators.
Prospective research students and research associates.
Universities and research institutions.

Scientific and industry partners.
The primary website language should be English. Bangla can be considered as a later addition if needed.

Main introduction

Our work brings together computational drug design, density functional theory, molecular docking, molecular dynamics simulation, network pharmacology, machine learning, and artificial intelligence to investigate molecular systems and support drug discovery research.
Primary buttons: Explore Our Research; View Publications. Secondary link: Collaborate With Us. Use one relevant scientific visual; a static image is sufficient initially.
Homepage sections

About preview: brief laboratory introduction with a Learn About the Lab link.
Featured publications: three to six selected records with title, authors, journal, year, DOI/publisher link, and optional approved thumbnail. Select highlights through the CMS.
Selected projects: approved summaries; hide if no content is ready.
People preview: selected current members and a link to the full directory.
Latest updates: the latest three news items, only if the laboratory will maintain this section.
Collaboration invitation: “We welcome inquiries about research collaboration in computational life sciences and drug discovery.” Button: Contact the Laboratory.
Footer: laboratory name, Dhaka, Bangladesh, approved email, navigation, approved professional/social links, privacy policy, and copyright.
Do not display an invented street address or map location.
6. About page
Laboratory overview: who we are, where we are based, computational research focus, and how our areas connect.
Suggested mission: To investigate molecular and biological systems through computational methods and develop research that supports drug discovery and advances understanding in the life sciences.
Suggested vision: To build a collaborative research environment connecting molecular modeling, computational chemistry, biological networks, and data-driven approaches.
Research approach: approved diagram or narrative showing how methods relate; do not imply every method is required in every project.
Optional verified content: establishment year, history, leadership, affiliation, partnerships, funding, and computing infrastructure.
7. Research page
Required themes: computational drug design; density functional theory; molecular docking; molecular dynamics simulation; network pharmacology; machine learning and AI in drug discovery.
For each theme include: a short accessible overview, scientific questions addressed, methods actually used, related projects, related publications, and an approved image or diagram where available.
Laboratory review is required before launch. Do not invent methods, software expertise, therapeutic areas, or experimental capabilities. Distinguish computational predictions from experimentally validated findings. Do not imply that a predicted compound is a proven medicine or that computation alone establishes clinical effectiveness.

9. Publications page
Display requirements
Newest publications first.
Year and research-theme filters; title/author search.
Clear distinction among journal articles, reviews, preprints, and other outputs.
DOI or publisher links.
Administrator-controlled homepage featuring.
Data fields
Title; complete author list; journal/venue; year; volume, issue, and pages/article number where applicable; DOI; publisher URL; publication type; publication status; research tags; related members; related project; optional abstract; optional approved figure; optional repository, code, and dataset links.
Editorial rules
Never invent a DOI or publication detail.
Do not label submitted or under-review manuscripts as published.
Label preprints clearly.
Identify work completed before a member joined so it is not misrepresented as laboratory-originated work.
Host PDFs only when the laboratory has permission to share that version.
Keep manual editing available even if DOI import is implemented.
Optional later features: BibTeX/RIS export and DOI-based metadata import.
10. Projects page
Each project should support: title, public summary, research theme, ongoing/completed status, approved team, publicly shareable methods, related publications, code/datasets, funding acknowledgment where applicable, and approved image.
Support draft and published states. Do not upload unpublished compound libraries, confidential partner information, sensitive datasets, or detailed unpublished results without authorization.
11. Opportunities and contact
Opportunities
Support research positions, student research opportunities, internships, and collaborations only where offered. Each listing needs a title, description, eligibility, expected skills, location/working arrangement, application instructions, deadline where applicable, and open/closed status.
Do not imply an opportunity is paid, funded, remote, or available unless confirmed. Initially, direct applications to an approved email instead of building an application portal.
Contact
Display the laboratory name, Dhaka, Bangladesh, approved email, exact address only if supplied for public use, professional links, and an optional inquiry form.
Form fields: name, email, institution/organization, inquiry subject, and message. Provide clear success/failure messages and delivery to the designated inbox.
Require server-side validation, not browser checks alone. Validate expected formats and intended use of submitted values. [S3]
Avoid public file uploads initially. If introduced, restrict file types and sizes, validate content, and implement safe storage and access controls. [S4]
12. Content management system
Provide a CMS so authorized members can update the site without code.
Manage people, publications, projects, news, and opportunities.
Change contact details and upload approved images.
Select homepage highlights and control ordering.
Save drafts and preview content.
Edit page titles and search descriptions.
Roles: Administrator for accounts, settings, and all content; Editor for approved editing/publishing permissions. Do not build a custom administration system without explaining why an existing CMS is insufficient.
Content relationships: People ↔ Publications ↔ Projects ↔ Research Themes. Allow editors to link records without repeatedly entering the same information.

The developer must propose a maintainable implementation and explain trade-offs.

Evaluate initial cost, annual hosting/subscriptions, update usability, technical support, backup/restoration, export/migration, and paid-theme/plugin/service dependence.
Initial scope is an informational website, not a computational platform. Exclude online docking/simulation submission, GPU/HPC integration, public user accounts, AI chatbot, online payments, learning management, and manuscript submission portals. Scope future computational services separately.
14. Accessibility, search visibility, and performance
Accessibility
Target WCAG 2.2 Level AA. Include keyboard navigation, visible focus, meaningful image alternatives, labeled fields, and appropriate headings. Minimum contrast is 4.5:1 for normal text and the applicable 3:1 threshold for large text. [S1]
Search visibility
Provide unique descriptive titles, clear headings, descriptive URLs, crawlable research/people/publication pages, suitable descriptions, an XML sitemap, and clear laboratory identity/location. These practices support discoverability but do not guarantee rankings. [S2, S5]
Suggested homepage title: Laboratory of Algorithmic Life Sciences | Dhaka, Bangladesh
Suggested description: Explore research in computational drug design, DFT, molecular docking, molecular dynamics, network pharmacology, and AI at the Laboratory of Algorithmic Life Sciences, Dhaka, Bangladesh.
Performance
Optimize image dimensions and sizes.
Avoid unnecessary plugins/scripts.
Load optional interactive elements only when needed.
Keep main content useful before decorative elements load.
Test mobile devices and a throttled connection.
Supply performance reports for the homepage and representative inner pages.
Agree on measurable targets before development, including testing tool, device profile, and network conditions.
15. Security, privacy, and ownership
HTTPS and protected administrator accounts.
Least-privilege access, spam protection, and submission rate limits.
Regular software updates.
Documented backup schedule, retention, and tested restoration.
No passwords or API keys in public code.
Privacy notice explaining contact-form data handling.
Agreed process for deleting inquiry data.
Documented maintenance responsibility after launch.
The laboratory must own or directly control the domain, hosting, CMS administrator access, source-code repository, content, analytics/search accounts if used, and paid themes/assets/subscriptions purchased for the project. The developer must not be the sole account owner.

19. Recommended initial launch scope
Prioritize Home, About, Research, People, Publications, Contact, and an easy-to-use CMS. Add Projects and Opportunities if approved content is available. Introduce News, Resources, interactive molecular viewers, or advanced features only with a clear purpose and a designated maintainer.
The central requirement is a scientifically accurate, well-organized website that the laboratory can update independently—not simply an attractive homepage.
